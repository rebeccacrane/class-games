Pick the Right Language: Answer Key

There's no single best programming language, only the best one for a particular job, team, and platform. These are strong answers, not the only answers. If your team picked something in the "Also arguable" column, or something else you can defend with a good reason, that counts.

SQL and HTML/CSS don't sit neatly on this line. With them, you describe what you want instead of giving step-by-step instructions. If your group set them aside, you noticed something real. The middle columns are a judgment call, so good evidence matters more than exact placement.

Stakes ratings

Mission critical People get hurt or serious money is lost.
Must be reliable It has to work, but a failure is fixable.
OK to fail or be slow It can crash, glitch, or run slowly and you just try again.

Stakes ratings are suggestions. Any rating your team can defend is fair, and some cards could go either way.

Think about it

  • The language wasn't really the choice: in I, J, K, N, and P, the platform, the existing code, or the team decided. What does that tell you about how languages get picked in the real world?
  • Q vs. H: both are about "fast." What does fast mean in each one?
  • T vs. Z: both are robots. Why does the rover get C and the science fair robot get Python?
  • W vs. X: both handle money data. Why does one need to be fast while the other can take an hour?
  • V vs. M: both are services running on the internet. What changes when thousands of customers depend on it?
  • We use Python in class. Which jobs would it be a poor choice for, and why?
